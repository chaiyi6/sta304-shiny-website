## About This Survey

### What we asked

This website presents the results of a short survey on how university students use **generative AI tools** (such as ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini) for their coursework, and how they feel about it. The survey was created by our group for STA304 (Surveys, Sampling and Observational Data) at the University of Toronto Mississauga in Fall 2026.

Students were asked:

- whether they had used generative AI for coursework, and how many hours a week they use it;
- what they mainly use it for (for example, exam preparation or writing);
- how much they agree with statements about its benefits (understanding concepts, working more efficiently) and its risks (incorrect information, reduced independent learning), on a scale from 1 to 7;
- how concerned they are about the risks of using AI for their own main purpose;
- what they see as its main benefit.

### Who answered

We received **34 responses**, collected through an online Google Form. One student did not answer whether they had used AI. All other answers used on this site are complete.

### How to use this site

- Use the **filters** in the sidebar to look at a smaller group of students, for example only students who use AI 8 or more hours a week, or only students who mainly use it for exam preparation. Every plot and explanation updates to match.
- Use the **tabs** to choose which result to look at, and the **drop-down menu** on the "Opinions on AI" tab to choose which question is shown.
- Use the **plot settings** to change the colour of the plots and how many hours are shown on the axis.
- The text under each plot explains what it shows in plain language.

Because the survey did not ask demographic questions such as age, gender or program, students are filtered by **how many hours they use AI each week** and **what they mainly use it for** instead.

Only one student chose coding and only one chose creative tasks as their main purpose, so these two answers are combined into a single "Other (Coding / Creative)" group on this site.

### Things to keep in mind

- **Small sample.** With only 34 students, results can shift noticeably if a few people had answered differently. When you filter down to a small group, this effect is even stronger, and the site will warn you when a group has fewer than 10 students.
- **Not a random sample.** Respondents were students we could reach (a convenience sample), not a random selection of all university students. The results describe the people who answered and may not represent students in general.
- **Self-reported answers.** Students estimated their own weekly hours and gave their own opinions, so answers may not be exact.
- **Ratings are opinions, not measurements.** A score of 6 out of 7 means a student agrees fairly strongly; it does not measure how much AI actually improved their work.
- **Patterns are not causes.** This is an observational survey. If two answers tend to go together, that does not mean one causes the other.

### Privacy

All responses are anonymous. Student identifiers and submission times were removed before the data were published on this site.
